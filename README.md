## Featured Projects

### [Child Health Risk Factors and Special Health Care Needs](https://github.com/darya-zhukova/child-health-risk-factors-cshcn)

Analyzed behavioral, psychosocial, environmental, and socioeconomic factors associated with special health care needs among children aged 6–17.

The project used weighted national survey data and included exploratory analysis, risk accumulation analysis, SQL data preparation, and interpretable logistic regression modeling.

**Tools:** Python, SQL, BigQuery, pandas, scikit-learn, Power BI

**Highlights:**

* analyzed 68,013 survey observations
* used survey weights representing approximately 47.5 million U.S. children
* identified low flourishing and adverse childhood experiences as strong risk signals
* achieved a weighted ROC-AUC of 0.74
* connected the findings to a parent-focused, non-diagnostic product concept

### [AdventureWorks Wholesale Customer Analysis](https://github.com/darya-zhukova/adventureworks-wholesale-customer-analysis)

Analyzed wholesale customer activity, revenue concentration, product performance, and regional retention patterns using the Microsoft AdventureWorks sample database.

The project focused on identifying high-value active customers, reactivation opportunities, geographic risk, and product strategies.

**Tools:** Power BI, DAX, data modeling, business intelligence

**Highlights:**

* segmented customers into Active, At-Risk, and Inactive groups
* identified the Top 10 Active customers contributing approximately 9.84% of wholesale revenue
* found the Southeast to have the highest inactive-customer share
* showed that Bikes generated high revenue despite lower order volume
* developed recommendations for retention, reactivation, and cross-selling

---

### [Subscription Retention Cohort Analysis](https://github.com/darya-zhukova/subscription-retention-cohort-analysis)

Built a weekly cohort retention analysis to understand customer churn during the first six weeks after subscription.

The project included cohort construction, incomplete-period handling, weighted retention calculations, and visual comparison across cohorts.

**Tools:** SQL, BigQuery, Google Sheets, cohort analysis

**Highlights:**

* calculated weekly retention from Week 0 through Week 6
* found the largest retention decline during the first two weeks
* showed weighted retention of approximately 88.5% by Week 6
* handled incomplete recent cohorts without treating unavailable periods as churn
* compared stronger and weaker cohorts to identify areas for further investigation
